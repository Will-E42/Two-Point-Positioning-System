Simple Two-Point Positioning System

Coordinate construction, triangulation, and trilateration using bearings, distances, and latitude/longitude coordinates.

Find A and B From My Position

Enter your latitude and longitude as the geographic reference point.
Require both bearings from YOU to landmarks A and B,
then, enter the distances from YOU to A and B,
or,
enter the A–B distance and either distance from YOU to A or to B.

Find My Position From A and B

Enter the latitude and longitude of A as the geographic reference point.
Require A-B distance and A → B bearing,
then, enter both bearings from A and B toward my position,
or,

How It Works

Geographic coordinate system
The calculator uses latitude and longitude as the real-world reference system. Internally, the geometry is calculated using a local East/North coordinate system. The local coordinates are then converted to latitude/longitude using the WGS84 Earth radius approximation.

Find A and B From My Position — distance method
Your latitude and longitude are used as the geographic reference point. The bearings determine the directions to A and B. Distances determine how far A and B are from you. Local East/North coordinates are calculated by coordinate construction and converted into latitude and longitude.

Find A and B From My Position — classic triangulation
Your latitude and longitude are used as the geographic reference point. The bearings determine the directions to A and B. The known A–B distance provides the third side of the triangle. If either YOU → A or YOU → B distance is also known, the missing distance can be solved using the Law of Cosines. Depending on the geometry, there can be zero, one, or two possible solutions.

Find My Position From A and B — classic trilateration
A is used as the geographic reference point. The A–B distance and A → B bearing determine the geographic position of B. With two bearings, the bearing lines are intersected to determine my position. Alternatively, bearings can be left blank and the distances from A and B to my position can be entered. The two distance circles are then intersected. Depending on the geometry, distance-only positioning can produce zero, one, or two possible positions.

Important geographic limitation
The internal calculation uses a local East/North tangent-plane approximation with a mean Earth radius. This is appropriate for relatively local positioning. For very large distances, especially hundreds or thousands of kilometres, Earth's curvature becomes significant and a full geodesic calculation should be used instead.

Bearings use the standard compass convention:
0° = North
90° = East
180° = South
270° = West
